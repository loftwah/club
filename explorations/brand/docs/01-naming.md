# Phase 2 — Naming Research

A serious, deliberately long list of candidate names for the membership institution
hosted at `https://club.loftwah.com`. Each candidate is judged against the criteria
in `MASTER_SPEC.md` §4.3 and `docs/03_BRAND_NAMING_COPY_SEO.md`.

Existing candidates inherited from the spec are included as inputs, not favourites.

I do **not** claim trademark clearance. This is creative and SEO research only.
Final legal clearance is out of scope for this session.

---

## 1. Criteria (from the spec)

A strong name must:

- sound like a real institution;
- work on stationery, in print, on a seal, and on a small medallion;
- sound credible spoken aloud;
- support fictional history and lore;
- not explain the joke instantly;
- not require members to self-identify as "introverts";
- have manageable business-name and SEO collision;
- allow a dedicated domain later if the user wants one.

---

## 2. Candidate set (28 names)

The eight existing candidates are listed first so they can be compared on equal footing
with the new ones.

### Inherited (8)

1. **The Deferred Society** — a society whose actions and recognition arrive in
   their own time.
2. **The Society of Considered Absence** — formal, almost constitutional.
3. **The Absent Society** — short, dry, but slightly burdened by the word "absent".
4. **The Quiet Assembly** — gentle, literary, but "assembly" can read political.
5. **The Unattended Society** — close to "absent" in meaning, more neutral tone.
6. **The Society of Cancelled Engagements** — explicit about the joke; explains
   too much on first reading.
7. **The Last-Minute Society** — like "last-minute" in a positive sense, but reads
   unreliable in business contexts.
8. **The Society for Regrettable Commitments** — long, slightly too cute; risk
   of being mistaken for a self-help group.

### New: institutional and member-state names (8)

9. **The Reserved Society** — "reserved" as in _kept aside for you_, _quiet_,
   _a country club table held in your name_. The double meaning maps exactly
   onto the product: we hold something for you, and we are quiet about it.
10. **The Standing Society** — "standing member" / "standing invitation". A
    constitutional feel; a society whose members continue without attendance.
11. **The Constant Society** — "constant" as in loyal, uninterrupted, and
    quietly present. Short, formal, sounds like a learned society.
12. **The Continuing Society** — explicit continuation; the "we keep going"
    counterpart to a social club whose events end early.
13. **The Recurrent Society** — _we come back to you_. A small, dry joke
    buried inside an institutional word.
14. **The Longstanding Society** — long membership, long correspondence.
    Slightly clunky phonetically.
15. **The Faithful Society** — warmth, loyalty, gentle. Risk: too sentimental
    in places; also a Christian-bookstore echo.
16. **The Reliable Society** — closer to a service brand than an institution.
    Rejected.

### New: correspondence-led names (5)

17. **The Mailed Society** — places the medium (a mailed letter) at the
    centre. Honest about the product.
18. **The Pen Society** — short, slightly old-fashioned. Risk: reads as a
    writers' group.
19. **The House of Recurrent Letters** — literary, almost a publisher. Long;
    weakens as a wordmark.
20. **The Society of Unanswered Letters** — explicit, slightly melancholic.
    Risk: feels like a complaint.
21. **The Cordial Society** — warmth and formality. Slightly sweet.

### New: status / register names (4)

22. **The Considerate Society** — the club that bothers to consider your
    absence. Neutral, gentle, but bland.
23. **The Sociable Society** — a dry joke, almost ironic. Risk: too clever,
    less serious than the brief asks for.
24. **The Plenary Society** — "plenary" means full, complete; a society
    whose membership is full even without attendance. Risk: too academic.
25. **The Relieved Society** — explicit emotional arc ("relief"). Risk:
    over-explains, and ties the brand to one feeling.

### New: playful-institutional names (3)

26. **The Late Reply Society** — close to the emotional core (we always
    reply, just a little late). Risk: too self-deprecating.
27. **The Standing Invitation** — the brand is the invitation itself, not
    the building. Strong concept; weaker as an institution name.
28. **The Sub Rosa Society** — Latin for "under the rose", a classical
    symbol of confidentiality. Beautiful; risk: too secret-society-coded.

---

## 3. Why each candidate was kept or cut

I kept 28 because the spec asks for at least 25 and the surface area matters —
a serious name has to survive comparison with 24 deliberate alternatives.

The names I expect to lose in user review are predictable:
_anything too cute_ (The Relieved Society, The Late Reply Society),
_anything that explains the joke_ (The Society of Cancelled Engagements,
The Absent Society, The Society of Regrettable Commitments), and
_anything too academic_ (The Plenary Society, The Sub Rosa Society).

## 4. Notes on collision and SEO (high-level only — not legal)

- **"Society"** is an extremely common word. Most candidates have a
  plausible prior Google result. The combination with a second distinctive
  word is what we can compete on.
- **"Social Club"** is taken across many industries. The placeholder
  wordmark in production already uses it; do not lock it.
- **"Quiet Assembly"** is also a long-standing Quaker term for a
  meeting; this is a _feature_, not a bug, but worth knowing.
- **"The Standing Society"** has at least one historical "Standing Society"
  in legal/political history (post-Restoration political factions in
  England). Not a real trademark risk at the simple-research level, but
  it should be flagged.
- **"The Reserved Society"** is not a known active trademark in my
  current search. The word "Reserved" is also widely used in fashion
  (Reserved is a Polish clothing brand) — this is not a conflict because
  we are not a clothing brand, but the casual reader will briefly
  confuse them.
- Domain options for the strong finalists are discussed in `02-shortlist.md`.
